# MissionForge: Every URL Is the Homepage

**The site:** missionforgehq.com
**Score: 61/100 - Grade D**

## First Impressions

This one is more serious than most: real analytics, bot protection, an actual backend. Someone cared about the plumbing. The mission, "change how people learn new skills," is a good one. The problems are in the layer you only notice when you leave the happy path.

## What I Found

**1. The phone sees a page 589px wide.** On a 390px screen the page scrolls 199px sideways. The nav row and a button are each about 412px, and the rest of the layout stretches to match. Mobile is where a lot of first visits to a learning product happen.

*Repro:* DevTools, 390x844, reload, drag sideways.

**2. Every URL is the homepage.** Ask for `/nope` and you get a 200 with the full homepage (about 30KB). Ask for `/favicon.ico` and you get the same HTML with a 200, not an icon. To a search engine that is an unlimited number of duplicate pages. To a visitor who mistypes a link, it is a page that looks right and is wrong.

*Repro:* Open `/nope`. Open `/favicon.ico`.

**3. The small print is too small to use.** The smallest text is 10 to 11px and the privacy link is 16px tall, under the 24px minimum for a tap target.

*Repro:* DevTools computed styles.

<Solution>
1. Find the container holding the nav and button and let it wrap or scroll inside itself (`overflow-x` on that element, `min-width: 0` on its flex children).
2. Return a real 404 status for unknown paths and serve an actual `favicon.ico`.
3. Raise the minimum font size to 12px and pad the privacy link to 24px high.
</Solution>

## What Works

The stack choices are sensible and the product has a point of view. This is a team that will fix these in an afternoon once they see them.

## Verdict

61/100. The foundation is better than the score. The score is the gap between a site that works for the person who built it and one that works for a stranger on a phone.

**Overall Score: 61/100 - Grade D**
