# Wendy Ji's Portfolio: Selling SEO Without Meta Tags

**Submitted by:** Wendy Ji (she asked for this)
**The site:** wendoj.vercel.app
**Score: 52/100**

## First impressions

Clean design. Dark theme, smooth animations, TypeScript and Next.js stated upfront. The layout is organized and the stack is right.

The catch is this: the portfolio is the product. Wendy built developer-portfolio, a template sitting at 175 GitHub stars and 58 forks. Her professional showcase is that template, deployed with her own information filled in. Anyone can clone it, swap in their details, and have an identical portfolio in about 20 minutes.

That's not inherently a problem. It proves the tool works. But it means the portfolio isn't evidence of design judgment or client work. It's a live demo of a template.

## The SEO problem

Wendy lists "SEO Optimization" in her services section.

Her own portfolio has no visible meta description. The title situation is unclear from what's crawlable.

Selling SEO while shipping a portfolio with no metadata is the kind of contradiction that surfaces fast in a client call. The fix isn't complicated: name plus role in the title tag, one sentence under 160 characters for the description, a canonical URL. One afternoon. A portfolio that sells SEO should be the example, not the counterexample.

## 15+ companies

This number sits on the homepage with nothing behind it.

No testimonials. No client names. No case studies.

Numbers without context are decoration. "15+ companies" invites a follow-up the portfolio can't answer. If the work is real, one concrete example beats ten abstract claims. Something like: built X for a client in Y industry, which resulted in Z. That's it. One example with an outcome changes the whole read.

## The projects

Four projects in the portfolio.

**RobloxScripts** is listed as a "script sharing platform for Roblox." Roblox script-sharing communities sit close to exploit and cheat tool territory. There may be something legitimate here, but a professional portfolio is not the place to leave that ambiguous. One sentence explaining what this actually was would save the reader from filling in the gap themselves.

**TranslateBot** and **Endalon** each get one line of description. That's not enough. What problem did they solve? What was the outcome?

**Wrona** is a real robotics technology company. This is probably the most interesting project on the list and there's almost nothing about what Wendy did there.

Also on GitHub, not in the portfolio: linkvertise-bypasser, a tool that routes around the ad monetization on Linkvertise links. It has 9 stars and it works. It doesn't belong in a freelance pitch.

## What works

The template itself is good work. 175 stars and 58 forks mean people actually use it. The animations are smooth without being distracting. The site loads fast. TypeScript throughout is the right call and the component structure in the public repo is readable.

Wendy can build things. The portfolio just doesn't show that as clearly as it should.

## Fixes

1. **Add a meta description.** One sentence, under 160 characters.
2. **Fix the title tag.** "Wendy Ji / Full-Stack Developer" is 35 characters. Done.
3. **Set a canonical URL.**
4. **Replace the "15+" stat with one real case study.** Name a project, describe the work, state an outcome.
5. **Add context to RobloxScripts.** What was it, actually? Who used it?
6. **Write two sentences about Wrona.** What did you build? What did it do?
7. **Add `rel="noopener"` to external links.**

**Time estimate:** One day. Mostly writing, not code.

## Verdict

52/100. The template is genuinely good. The portfolio as a professional document isn't doing Wendy any favors.

Fix the SEO on the site that sells SEO. Replace the number with a story. Be more deliberate about which projects belong in a freelance pitch versus a GitHub feed. The technical ability is there. The presentation just needs to show it.

**Overall Score: 52/100**
